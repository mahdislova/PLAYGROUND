<!-- OWN-08: Aftermath, mercy, the prisoner, the march down, a second escape attempt -->

"Well done, milord," said Jarvis, one of the scouts who'd evinced enough nerve to play bait over the course of this long week.

Hogweed stood to the side, her face twisted into pity and disgust.

A pity and disgust that was mirrored in Hugh's heart. The ram girl was mewling in distress, twisting and turning gently in his arms as if clutched by a nightmare.

For long, uncertain moments he simply held her, and then he crouched and set her down on the ground.

"Back," he commanded, and the ring of torches and curious flames retreated a few paces.

"Back!" snapped Hugh, glaring at the bandits, who retreated further, finally clearing the area.

"Can you hear me?" Hugh stared down into the ram girl's face. Her painted eyelids were flickering, her eyes rolling, trying to turn her head from side to side but prevented from doing so by her horns.

"Damnit," he hissed. "Hogweed. A rope."

To her credit, the Poison Sister didn't question or simply repeat his words back to him, but nodded and ducked into the scout's tent, emerging a second later with a coil of tarred, slender cord.

"Fashion it into a slipknot," said Hugh. "Hurry."

Welts were forming around the ram girl's wrists and about her neck, as if the metal were actually burning her.

Hogweed's fingers moved rapidly, and she extended a noose to Hugh. Who slipped it around the ram girl's neck, tightened it, and then moved his fingers to her iron collar.

"Wait," said Jarvis, voice taut with panic. "If you release her, won't her, you know, her powers… how can we be sure…?"

Hugh fixed the man with a glare that drove him back a few paces. Pointed at the moaning girl. "This isn't who we are, no matter what war we wage."

And so saying, he drew the pin from the collar, pulled it free, and cast it aside. A moment later the manacles followed suit.

The fae's face contorted into a deep frown, but she ceased her moaning. A moment later her eyelids flickered, and then her hazel eyes locked on Hugh who knelt beside her.

She froze, like a deer startled by a hunter stepping out from behind a tree.

Hugh spread his hands wide. "No iron. I didn't know the effect it would have. I'm sorry for using it."

Her faun's ears twitched, flicked, rotated, but she didn't otherwise move, her entire frame betraying terrible tension.

"I'm going to use this rope instead," said Hugh, lifting the cord. "Because you're still technically our prisoner. We're taking you down to Erro. I promise on my honor to not harm you if you don't seek to escape."

"You have no honor," spat the girl, her voice surprisingly rich and mellifluous.

"I don't need to convince you of that. I'll let my actions speak for me. Will you promise to not try to escape if I only bind you with rope?"

"No," she said, shifting now to sit, turning her head from side to side as she examined the bandits who ringed them both.

Hugh sighed. "Really? You want me to place the manacles and collar back on?"

She darted a glance at where the iron lay, and looked away as if the very sight seared her eyes.

"Never mind. Even without your word I'll not put those on you. But I promise you this: try to escape, and I will hunt you down." His words rang with grim certainty. "You cannot escape me, no matter how fleet-footed you think you are."

She turned back to him, the authority in his words capturing and holding her attention. Her ear flicked again, but her brows lowered. "What are you?"

"The name's Hugh of Stasiek." He rose to his feet, back twinging in pain from where she'd blasted him. Blood had caused his tunic to cling to his chest. The thrown knives had fallen free, and had fortunately been small, but pain yet radiated from where they'd stabbed into his hip, his thigh, his side.

"My lord?" Hogweed. "You maybe want those bandaged before we set off?"

Hugh considered it. "No. I want us gone before the fae return with reinforcements. The sooner the better. Good work, everybody. Hogweed, let's quit this camp and take everything of value with us."

The Poison Sister took control, her commands sharp and on point, and in moments men were collapsing the tent, dousing the fire, collecting supplies, climbing out of the goblet canyon, bows at the ready.

"Your name?" asked Hugh, staring down at the ram girl.

She returned his gaze with a defiance that couldn't quite mask her fear.

"Very well," said Hugh. "We'll go with Goat Girl for now."

Anger flickered across her features, her flattened nose wrinkling in distaste, and he thought for a second she'd contradict him, her pride forcing her to give up her true name, but instead she looked sharply away.

"Up," he said. "I don't want to, but I can drag you all the way down the mountain if you insist."

Reluctantly, she rose to her feet. She was of medium height, just a little taller than Morwyn, but her willowy frame was impossibly feminine; he could have encircled her waist with both hands, but her bosom was large, her hips wide. Her crimson hair fell down past her shoulders nearly to her waist, and only then did he realize he was staring; the ram girl was all too aware, her expression having hardened, and with an apologetic grimace Hugh looked away.

Their flight down the mountain was uninterrupted; they made good time, the ram girl clearly contemplating escape the whole way but unable to formulate a plan, not with Hugh walking behind her and Hogweed's men encircling them.

It was just shy of midnight when they broke free of the trees and Erro came into view, the Mandroga a pewter line beneath the waning moon, the houses dark but for the lit windows of the tavern.

Hugh looked to where Hogweed marched just ahead. "Who's on patrol?"

"Snowdrop."

"Her men better intercept us," he said. "We're already closer than -"

"My lord." A slender figure stepped out of the shadows beside an outcropping of rock, her bow held down by her side.

"Ah. Snowdrop." Hugh gave her a wolfish grin. "I was just complimenting your vigilance."

The young Poison Sister inclined her head with obvious wry amusement, but her gaze was fixed on the ram girl. "Success at last?"

"We're about to find out. I'm going to send out another patrol to reinforce yours. Expect retribution."

"Very well." She stepped back. "I'll pass word on to the men."

The closer they drew to Erro the more frantic the ram girl became. She began stalling, forcing Hugh to push her forward, sending her stumbling down the path, her feet seeming to have turned to blocks of wood. When they were close enough to hear the rushing whisper of the falls, she let out a pained cry and threw herself to the side, charging toward the path's embankment.

Hugh cursed and pulled back on the rope; he'd forgotten just how powerful her charge could be, and was dragged forward, heels skidding across the rocky path as she lowered her head to bull her way up the side of the bank.

Hogweed's men cried out in alarm, and more than one raised a nocked arrow in her direction.

Foughtash yet rode within Hugh, along with three other Reavers, but it wasn't enough; terror gave the girl unnatural strength, and Hugh staggered and slid after her, unable to arrest her flight.

For a moment he considered summoning others, but then in a flash he leaned into the skills of those who already rode him; calling on Evassier and Akilina's dexterity, he ceased hauling back and sprang forward instead, leaping onto the girl's back, wrapping an arm about her neck once more, and bore her to the ground.

She screamed in frustration and horror, reared up onto her knees, but Hugh regained his balance and hauled back, holding her tight, arching her back like a bow.

"Stop!" he hissed into her ear as she clawed at his forearm, her crimson hair in disarray, her head twisting from side to side once more in an effort to gouge him. "You cannot escape me, stop!"

She gave one last, heart-rending cry of desolate fear, and then sagged in his arms, the strength seeping out of her.

"On my honor," panted Hugh, holding her tight against him. "On my honor, you will not be harmed." He didn't even know what he was saying, the words tumbling free from his lips as if on their own accord. "You won't be harmed. You won't be tortured. I swear it."

But for her trembling she remained still, her back yet arched, her one horn pressed into his bloody shoulder, her hands at his forearm, her chest rising and falling as she panted.

Hugh relaxed his grip slowly. "I swear it," he whispered, allowing his arm to fall away. "I swear it."

She curled forwards, as if bowing to the distant forest, and her shoulders shook with silent tears. Hugh rose to his feet and stared down at her, feeling monstrous, but unable to interrupt her grief. Watched and waited as she shuddered and wept, the bandits and Hogweed watching in wonder and silence, until at last the girl mastered herself and rose, wiping an arm across her eyes.

"Very well," she whispered. "Let us see what your honor is worth, Hugh of Stasiek."

Throat tight, Hugh nodded. What use more words? Hogweed signaled, her men resumed their descent, and their group marched the remaining distance to Erro proper.

"Send word to whomever is in camp to go on patrol with Snowdrop," he commanded Hogweed as they drew close to his estate. "And put the rest of the camp on alert. I want two patrols in Erro proper, one on the rooftops, the second watching the entrances, both southern and north. We're to remain on war footing till well past dawn."

"Yes, my lord," said Hogweed, voice curt. She turned to leave, taking a branching path away from his own toward the bandit camp, but stopped when he called to her.

"And Hogweed?"

She looked back, suspicious.

"Well done."

If anything, his praise seemed to deepen her suspicion, so that with a dubious nod she led her men away to Floriana's camp.

"This way," he said to the ram girl, leading her along the path to the imperial estate.

Light shone out from the cracks between the shutters. Hugh reached the entrance, and paused. Took a deep breath, glanced at the quiescent fae, and then pushed the door open and entered.
