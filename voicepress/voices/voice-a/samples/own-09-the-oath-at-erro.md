"Hugh!" said Zarja, looking up from where she was reading a book on the dining table, a candle set close by. "You're -"

Morwyn sat in another chair, leaning back on the two rear legs, carefully rebinding the hilt of her blade. Anastasia was in the open kitchen, in the act of pouring steaming water into a teapot.

All three froze as the ram girl entered behind Hugh, her horns nearly brushing the doorframe on either side.

"- back," finished Zarja, rising to her feet, dropping her Elena guise immediately to appear in her lisica form, her golden hair rising into curls, her ears slipping forth, her eyes gleaming as if with their own inner fire.

The ram girl seemed no less shocked to see her, eyes widening, ears going rigid, her whole frame tensing in surprise.

For a moment neither spoke, and their silence bound everyone else's lips. Then the ram girl said a phrase in the native fae tongue, her voice betraying confusion and query.

Zarja responded, tone guarded.

Her words hit the ram girl like a fist, causing her to actually step back. She fired back a question, her tone rife with incredulity.

Zarja spread her hands, expression solemn, dignified, and responded with calm and certainty.

Silence, and then the ram girl spat on the floor.

"Well, that seems to have gone well," said Morwyn.

Sorrow flickered across Zarja's face, but it never touched the resolve in her eyes. "It's hard to explain," she said, speaking still to the ram girl. "But I sense the hand of fate behind Hugh. Where he goes, the world will change and our people shall be changed with it. I have chosen to stand by his side in the hopes of helping him understand our people, and in doing so ameliorate the cruelty of our fate."

The ram girl laughed, the sound derisive and mocking. "I see. So your influence leads to my being captured with ropes instead of iron. You change the decorations, but not the outcome."

"No," said Hugh, dropping the rope and moving to the closest bottle of wine. "Her influence leads me to capturing you instead of simply leading five hundred men armed with fire and iron into the mountains to slay every living fae."

The ram girl shuddered, darted a look at Zarja, who gave her a grim nod.

"You wish to make another traitor of me," spat the girl. "For me to betray my people so you can march up there with not only fire and iron, but with information to make your battle all the easier."

"I don't want you to betray your people," said Hugh, pulling the cork from the wine bottle with his teeth and spitting it aside. "I want you to betray those corrupted by Arasim. Your people, the true fae, I wish…"

He paused. Foughtash and the others yet lurked within him. Could they hear his words? How close could he come to saying what was in his heart without Jacinia learning of his intent?

Without giving her sufficient cause to pluck his life and cast him into the Ashen Gardens along with the other gladsome Reavers?

"…yes?" prompted the ram girl.

"To be more judicious," he allowed at last.

The ram girl sneered. "Judicious. A fine word that does not mask your true intent."

Hugh filled a goblet, drained it, refilled it.

"Be easier to just drink from the bottle," said Morwyn.

"That would be uncouth."

Anastasia moved forward, a steaming cup in hand. "Please, for better or worse, you are our prisoner for now. Sit. Have some tea."

The ram girl tossed her head proudly, sweeping her hair back from her shoulders. "I may be your prisoner, but I will never act as your guest."

"Fine," said Hugh. "So let us get to it. I swore to you on my honor you would not be hurt. I will hold to that pledge. Come dawn, if you desire it, I will set you free. This I also swear on the love Fortuna has for me."

"Free?" asked Morwyn.

"Free," said Hugh firmly. "I'll kill anyone who moves to harm me and mine, strike them down in combat, but this? No. My memories of how I was treated by Katharzina are too fresh. I'll not torture or imprison."

"Hard to argue with that," said Morwyn, and resumed winding leather around the bare wood of the hilt. "Though it's probably the least effective start to an interrogation I've ever heard."

Anastasia set the cup of tea before an empty chair. "My name is Anastasia, a disciplus of Duke Annaro and currently in service to Lord Hugh. This is Morwyn, the captain of the duke's personal guard, and this is Zarja, our friend and companion."

"Zarja Lythgraefen el-Abeian," said the lisica softly.

The ram girl stared down her flattened nose at them all. "I've no interest in your names. To me you are merely the enemy."

"That so." Hugh sat on the edge of the table, placing his boot on the closest chair, leaning forward with one elbow on a knee. "Interesting. So you're all for demon worship, for perverting the true nature of the fae, for doing whatever it takes to bring a new war upon humanity, even if it means drowning the world in fire and blood so that everyone loses in the end?"

"Humanity must perish!" The ram girl's voice rang out, fierce and proud. "This village, this valley, was once my home! Before you split the trees, polluted the streams, before you tore the stones from the earth with which to build your barrows, we fae lived here, harming nobody, cherishing the wild, nurturing beast and bird! This valley, and the valley beside it, the rolling lowlands, the plains beyond! This world was ours, and wherever the eye fell one saw marvels, one saw beauty, one saw harmony as nature flourished under our stewardship! Now?"

Hugh sipped from his wine, not bothering to answer her clearly rhetorical comment.

"Now? The land is covered in wheat and barley, a single crop that has supplanted the abundance that once grew everywhere. Forests are leveled, animals slaughtered, richness replaced by an endless herd of cattle to feed your hunger! The sky chokes with smoke, your cities reek of filth and excrement, and you - you don't even live in harmony with one another! All you desire is power and money, and never are you sated, never your ambitions quenched! You tread on each other in your vaulting quest for ever more, more than you can consume in your short lives, sitting your barrows of stone, hoarding glittering gold like petty dragons, glorying in the misery of your fellow humans as they groan and die of disease in the streets!"

Her eyes flashed, her chest heaved, and she lashed all of them with her glare.

"Your world is one of filth and death, blood and slaughter, greed and hatred! In three short centuries you have perverted our land beyond measure, and worse! You have driven us from our ancestral homes, have thrown us into ever remote corners of the world, yet even that is insufficient! It is not enough that we live out of your sight, but you must hunt us down, you must kill us, and why?"

Her hands were clenched, and now pain cracked through her fury. "Why do you do this? Even today, long after our bravest warriors are dead, after our cruelest leaders killed, when all that remains of our people wish for nothing but peace and to try and scrape by on the scraps you have left us, why do you still hunt us? Do we offend you so with our very existence? Can you not sleep atop your beds of gold in peace, knowing that out there, somewhere, the rightful owners of this land yet draw breath?"

Hugh finished his cup of wine, but the power of her words stayed his hand. He made no move to refill it, nor did Morwyn work on her hilt. Everyone listened, mesmerized.

"I know why. We know why," said the ram girl, looking to Zarja with stony anger. "Because we are the embodiment of your guilt. You cannot enjoy your stolen goods in peace as long as we live. You must kill us off, even the most gentle of our kind, the most innocent, so that you need no longer be reminded of your crimes. Only then will you be able to sink into your perfidy without any pangs of conscience. Only then will you be able to smile at your bloody hands without horror. Only when every fae lies dead will you be able to lie to yourselves and say this land was always yours, its riches always yours to reap, and never need fear being contradicted."

Hugh pursed his lips and nodded thoughtfully, finally refilling his goblet. "You're right."

The ram girl's face, which had grown flush with the passion of her words, turned pale. "You admit it, then? You've so little shame?"

"I do." Hugh sipped from the wine, not tasting anything but ashes in his mouth. "I've known that blind hatred myself. Blind, I say, because when I killed your kind it was because I was taught that this was right, this was good, and the world would be a safer place without you."

The ram girl went to interject but Hugh spoke on.

"But I no longer feel that hate. I've parted company with those who do. But I still understand that hatred, and disagree with it. I will have no part in it. As I said before, my quarrel here is not with fae such as yourself. It is with those Vispathia has led into the shadow of Arasim. Those who have taken demon taint into their spirits so as to be able to strike back against my people."

The ram girl sneered. "I am to believe this?"

"Yes." And though the Reavers yet rode within him, Hugh felt a weariness descend upon him that had nothing to do with his physical labors. "I can't claim to understand your pain. No human can. But I am a man of no small power. I have given you my word that you shall go free come dawn. One way or another I will kill the demon-tainted, and then, Erro secured, I shall return to Stasiek to confront my brother. And I shall demand that he lift the ban on fae who reside in these valleys, allowing them to return to their homes if they are willing to share the land with the people who already live here."

"Hugh!" Anastasia's shock was palpable. "You can't be serious."

"The Fate Makers will have your head," said Morwyn quietly. "And your brother's, if he defies them."

"They can try and take it," said Hugh. "I'm not an easy man to stop."

"You cannot defy the empire's entire religion," said Anastasia, speaking slowly with shocked wonder as if she were trying to wrap her mind around what he was proposing. "Every baron, every duke, the imperial army itself, would march upon Stasiek."

"Then we'll die," said Hugh. "It's no great thing, death. I may die for saying these words alone. But I grow tired."

"Tired?" asked Anastasia, voice rich with disbelief once more.

"Tired of committing crimes simply because I'm ordered to do so."

Anastasia ran her hands through her hair, disrupting her chignon without care. "And so you will set yourself against the Fate Makers? The Emperor? The Disciplus Academy - all of Mendev will rise."

"Then let them."

"You can't be serious," said Morwyn, voice flat.

"I am." Hugh drained his goblet, tossed it aside, rose and strode to the shuttered window. Turned. "Because the more time I spend in your company - all of you, the three of you - the more I come to value integrity. Laugh if you will. I care not. But Zarja is teaching me to look beyond appearances. Anastasia, you are teaching me the value of independence from the institutions that have molded my life. And Morwyn, from you I am learning how to be true to myself, no matter what that self may look like. How can I take those lessons, the love you have given me, and return to life in the empire after this, with all its blind cruelties and stupidities?"

Nobody spoke.

"The answer is, I can't. Nor will I live in the wild like a bandit. Nor can I simply walk away, as I once planned, to explore the boundaries of the maps, in so doing abdicate my responsibilities to this empire, to its peoples, to the crimes I myself committed. No. I will defy the heavens themselves if I must, but I'll put a stop to the injustices that rule the day, and if I die for it, then - well. I die. But I will die myself, Hugh of Stasiek, a man who was worthy, if only for a few short years, of your love."

Zarja's smile was tremulous as she clasped her hands together over her heart. Anastasia opened her mouth and then snapped it shut again. Morwyn was frowning at him as if he were a problem she couldn't solve with the edge of her blade.

"Think on it. None of us can return to life as it was. Anastasia. You and I have concocted plans to escape, to escape the Academy, to escape Mendev itself. Morwyn, can you honestly say you can return to Annaro's service as his captain, and content yourself to a life of executing his commands with bloodless efficiency?"

Nobody spoke. Anastasia chewed her bottom lip and looked away. Morwyn continued to stare darkly at him.

"So why not? Why not plant our flag and defy the world to come crush us? We'll die, I know we will, but what of it? I'd rather die for a cause I believe in, die beside the women I love, than bend knee to corruption, greed, everything Goat Girl here just spoke of. Because everything she said about humanity was correct, or close enough, and I'll have no part of it any longer."

"Oreanna," said the ram girl, voice soft.

Hugh stared blankly at her.

"Oreanna," she said again, then blushed. "Not Goat Girl."

"Oreanna, then, thank you." Hugh took up the near empty bottle of wine, went to drain it, but turned to Morwyn and Anastasia instead. "Well? What say you?"

"Death," said Morwyn, voice sepulchral. "It's a guarantee. We've no chance of surviving this madness. Our bodies will be feeding the crows before the year is out."

"Agreed," said Anastasia. "I can attest firsthand how the Academy will react. You're asking us to die, Hugh."

"Fine. Die with me." He felt wild, liberated, uncontainable. "But for however long we live, we four shall burn so brightly that the sun itself, the moon and stars, shall stare down at us in envy. Because I can tell you this: most of the years I've lived thus far have been wasted, drowned in alcohol and blood. I've felt such misery for most of my life that I'm not glad to have lived those years at all, and would trade them all, trade each sordid, pitiful moment, for months by your side. Months where I can stand tall as a man, where I can stare at my own reflection and know that I am my own person, that I own my fate, and that nobody can break me. Kill me, yes. But break me? With you by my side? With your love in my heart? Never. So yes. Come die with me. Come live with me. For a few months, maybe a year, we shall defy the odds, and for as long as we can we will celebrate what it means to be good, to be true, to be in love, and when that ax falls, as I know it must, then I will go to the Ashen Gardens with a glad heart, with no regrets and a smile upon my bloodied lips."

The silence that followed was so intense that it practically ached.

"And that," said Zarja, voice soft, "is why I am with him. That is why I am willing to risk all to stand by his side."

Anastasia stepped forward, placed her arms upon his shoulders, and eyes gleaming, smiled up at him. "Your madness is… is utterly infectious," she whispered, voice husky, and then rose to her tiptoes to kiss him on the lips.

"Madness indeed," said Morwyn, voice sullen. "But fuck it. It's not as if I had much to look forward to. A life serving Annaro's whims seems desperately boring at this point. So fine. Let's tell the world to go burn and see how many souls we can send to the Ashen Gardens as our vanguard."

Anastasia's lips quirked into a smile against his own, and then she pulled away to turn and stare at Morwyn, one arm still around Hugh's neck. "Go on. Say you love us, that you'll die for us, and can't imagine being happy anywhere else."

Zarja laughed, and even Hugh couldn't repress a smile when Morwyn scowled and looked away.

But then Zarja's smile slipped away. "What about your… responsibilities?"

Hugh grimaced. "I'm going to have to figure that out."

"What responsibilities?" asked Oreanna.

Anastasia smoothed down her slate blue and gold-trimmed uniform. "To his brother, I imagine. The duke."

"I'll deal with my brother when the time comes," said Hugh. "And if he doesn't see matters as I do, then I'll react accordingly."

"What of Floriana's people?" asked Morwyn. "We need them to fight against Arasim's corruption."

"And Niestor," added Anastasia. "We're going to need his alliance if we're to succeed. None of them will fight alongside you if they know the truth."

"Then we'll have to tell them when the moment is right," said Hugh. "There's idealism and then there's idiocy. We need to show them the truth of the matter first, let them see that the fae are not our enemies. Once they have seen this for themselves, only then will I tell them what we intend."

Nobody seemed convinced.

"And if they don't agree? We'll just kill them all, won't we, my Lord?" asked Morwyn with feigned cheer.

Hugh snorted. "It won't come to that. But no. Telling everyone right now what we intend before they can even understand it is foolishness. Just as Zarja only revealed herself to you two once you'd grown to trust and love Elena."

"Elena?" asked Oreanna.

Zarja responded with a fluid stream of fae, and the ram girl nodded when she was done.

"Very well," said Anastasia. "I feel almost light-headed. Perhaps I need stronger stuff than tea."

"Never drink tea when you can have wine," said Hugh.

"But where do we go from here?" The disciplus took Hugh's empty bottle from his fist, inspected it with a frown, then stepped toward the kitchen. "Our next step?"

"That depends on Oreanna," said Hugh, sitting on the edge of the table once more. "And what she's willing to share with us about Arasim's corruption."

The ram girl hesitated. Which was a huge improvement, thought Hugh. He watched her. Watched her uncertainty, how her hazel eyes flicked from one woman to another, resting on him like a restless bird before flickering away again.

To rest, at last, as he'd known it would, on Zarja.

Oreanna said something in her mellifluous native tongue, something that sounded halfway between the call of a bird at dawn and the trill of a stream over rocks.

Zarja replied in kind, her grace, her gravity, giving her an almost royal aura.

Oreanna pondered, asked something else.

What Hugh wouldn't have given to understand fae.

Zarja's response was immediate, authoritative, and clearly was about Hugh, for both women turned to stare at him.

"Whatever it was, I didn't do it," said Hugh, raising his goblet to his lips and finding it empty. "Most like."

"I don't understand," said Oreanna. "I've never met - never heard - of a human like yourself."

"He's unique in more ways than one," said Anastasia, her smile warm.

Morwyn snorted.

"Look, as much as I like being the center of a compliment party, there's no need. I'm fully aware of how wonderful I am -"

Oreanna raised one of her upswept brows.

"Oh, prepare yourself," said Morwyn. "He's nigh insufferable once he gets going."

"- but this is about trust. Words are pretty, but we've all heard orators who promise gold and deliver poison. So you tell me, Oreanna. What more can I do to prove myself? To prove I only mean ill for those who are demon-tainted?"

Her faun-like ears flicked as she considered him. Despite her young appearance she was manifesting just the same gravity and poise as Zarja; perhaps all fae were able to appear so dignified and poised when they wished.

"Very well." Her tone grew sharp. "You ask me to trust you with the lives of my friends and family. Let me see you trust me in turn."

"All right. How?"

"Remove this rope from my neck, give me a knife, and let me place it at your neck."

"No," said Morwyn. "Absolutely not."

"Done," said Hugh.

Zarja clearly wanted to say something, but at the last bit her lower lip and remained silent.

"Come here," said Hugh.

Oreanna frowned, not liking his peremptory tone, perhaps. So Hugh sighed. "Come here, if you please."

The ram girl stepped forward. Almost Hugh expected to hear the clip clop of delicate hooves, but glancing down, he saw two bare, human feet.

He drew his dagger and with his free hand pressed two fingers under her chin. Her skin was smooth, warm, and this close he could smell her scent, a verdant smell like that of the deep forest. Slowly, gently, he tipped her chin up, elongating her throat, and slid his blade under the cord, the flat of the dagger easing across her skin.

Goosebumps broke out across her neck, down her neckline. She stopped breathing, and for a moment he simply kept the blade against her neck, fingers under her chin, till with a savage slash he cut the cord away and the noose fell to the ground.

She startled, stepped back, to be confronted by the hilt of his dagger.

"Here," he said. "I'm strong, but there's not much I can do with a slashed throat."

"Hugh," said Morwyn, rising to her feet and sending her chair crashing to the ground. "This is utter madness. You've known her for less than half an hour. I understand what you're trying to do, but this is too dangerous -"

"Same goes for her," said Hugh, keeping his voice steady, his gaze locked on Oreanna's hazel eyes. "We're asking her to risk everything. If I can't do the same, I'm not worthy of her trust."

He reached out, wrapped his fingers around her slender wrist, and raised the dagger to his throat.

Felt the blade scritch over his stubble.

"Right here," he said, voice soft. "Press and pull. You'll slash open my artery. I'll bleed out over the floor and be dead in less than a minute."

Oreanna's eyes were wide. Her youthful face pale, her beauty made all the more striking for possibly being the last thing he'd see. Her chest rose and fell rapidly, and he felt her put pressure on the blade, felt it press into his skin.

"I kill you, your attack on Vispathia founders," she whispered, moving closer. "I kill you, this camp falls into chaos. Buying us time until someone comes to replace you."

A second blade insinuated itself through her hair, its point emerging as it slid along her neck.

"You kill him," said Morwyn, "and you'll not draw another breath."

"I was prepared to die for the court when I took up my bow," whispered Oreanna. "Prepared to give my life for my people."

"Morwyn," said Hugh. "Back down."

"No," said Morwyn.

"That's a direct order from your commanding officer," said Hugh. "Back down, captain."

Morwyn's expression crumpled into helpless rage and fury, but her blade retracted, barely disturbing the crimson locks through which it had appeared.

"Now," said Hugh, breathing in deep, slow breaths. "We've come to it. Strike for Vispathia and Arasim, or pull that blade from my neck and help us cleanse the taint from your people."

"You are mad," said Oreanna, searching his face with something akin to dawning wonder. "Or the most manipulative bastard I have ever had the misfortune to meet."

"I'd attest for his honesty," said Anastasia, "but you've no reason to trust me, either."

"I'll attest to his idiocy," said Morwyn, "and know you'd agree with me."

"I vouch for him," said Zarja, moving around the table to stand beside Hugh. Her tail waved hypnotically from side to side, and her eyes fair blazed with gold light. "I, Zarja Lythgraefen el-Abeian, who stood beside White Pelleas at the battle of Golden Ford; I, Zarja Lythgraefen el-Abeian, who was loved by the flame incarnation Amarinth and loved him in turn; I, Zarja Lythgraefen el-Abeian, who walked the Inner Glade before it was burned, who danced at al'Thaneaos before it was toppled, who bathed in the Emerald Pool before it was fouled."

She reached out, slowly, oh so slowly, and laid her hand on Oreanna's wrist. Exerted no pressure, but simply touched her.

"I, Zarja Lythgraefen el-Abeian, who now loves Hugh of Stasiek, and believe in his heart, in his dreams, and his future. I vouch for him."

Oreanna's eyes swam with tears. The blade trembled at Hugh's throat. Her lips trembled, and then with a cry she pulled the dagger away, allowed it to fall from her nerveless fingers, and covered her face as she turned away, nearly taking Hugh's throat out with the tip of one horn.

Zarja moved smoothly to slide an arm around the ram girl's shoulders, to pull her into an embrace. She spoke softly to her in the language of the fae as more sobs wracked the young woman, held her and nodded to Hugh, indicating he should give them space.

Hugh's heart felt like an anvil upon which Fortuna herself was smiting ore, each blow of her hammer resonating through his body and soul. Eyes glazed, mouth dry, he walked backward into the kitchen, to pull a bottle of wine from a crate. Tore the cork free with just his fingers, plucking it forth with a loud pop, and placed the mouth to his lips.

Drank, drank with long, powerful, deliberate pulls, until his stomach was filled with wine.

Watched and made no complaint as Zarja led Oreanna into the large bedroom at the back of the estate.

As Morwyn righted her chair and sat back down, shaking her head in wonder.

Anastasia rubbed at her face, inhaling sharply, and then followed Hugh into the kitchen. Made to take the bottle, thought twice, and plucked one of her own forth.

"One day, Hugh of Stasiek," said Morwyn, voice low, tone hard. "That kind of shit is going to get you killed."

Hugh finished the last of the bottle and pulled it away with a sigh. "Probably. But this is not that day."

Wrong, whispered a voice in his ear.

His blood ran cold. Was that Akilina?

"Hugh?" Morwyn's attention was total, and she was already half out of her chair. "What is it?"

Come, my lord. Akilina's voice in truth. Come answer to your crimes.

And like that his mind was plucked forth, his consciousness dragged down into his core, deeper, into the very nadir of his soul, and the world went dark as it fell away.
