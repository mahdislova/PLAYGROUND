Rain had found its way down the back of Kessa's collar an hour ago and was now somewhere around the small of her back, making itself at home. She sat her horse at the near end of the toll bridge at Harrowgate and studied the gatehouse.

Somebody had paid a fortune for that gatehouse. Dressed stone, fresh tar on the portcullis chains, the Margrave's boar painted over the arch in gold leaf that had never seen a winter. Then they had paid nothing at all for the bridge itself, which sagged in the middle and was missing planks on the downstream side. New money, and frightened of something.

Interesting.

The guard on the gate was perhaps twenty, all elbows inside a coat of borrowed mail, and he held his halberd the way a boy holds a girl at his first dance.

"Toll's a silver," he said.

"It was a copper last spring."

"Last spring there wasn't a war on," he said, and shifted his grip on the halberd, which didn't help.

"There isn't one now."

"There will be." He said it with enormous satisfaction, as though he had arranged it personally. "Silver, or you swim. River's high, mind."

Kessa considered the river, which was brown and fast, full of branches, and then considered the boy.

"Tell me," she said. "Does the Margrave know you're charging a silver? Or does he think it's still a copper, and you've a very nice pair of boots on the way?"

He went red to the ears. Glanced down at his boots before he could stop himself. They were very nice boots.

"A silver," he said again, but quieter.

So he wasn't a thief, only a boy who'd discovered arithmetic. She could work with that.

"Here's a copper," said Kessa, and flicked it to him. "And here's another, for you to forget you ever saw me. If anyone comes asking after a woman with a sword and a bad temper -"

"Never seen one," he said quickly, and swung the gate wide.
