The first of them came over the bar, and Brennic broke his wrist on the beer tap and put him back behind it.

The second had a cudgel and more confidence than reach. Brennic stepped inside the swing and set a forearm under his chin, and the man sat down hard on a stool that hadn't been expecting him and went over backward with it. The other two came together, which was clever of them, so he threw the nearer one's own pint in his friend's face, and while one was blinking and the other was mourning his ale he put them both through the front window.

The glass held. They didn't.

After that it was quiet in the taproom, apart from the fire popping and somebody's dog barking two streets over. The fifth man stood alone in the middle of the floor with a knife in his hand and the expression of someone doing sums and not liking the total.

"Well?" said Brennic.

The man looked at his friends in the street. Looked at the knife, as though it had let him down personally.

"Who sent you?"

Nothing. The man's eyes went to the door and back, measuring it.

"Who?"

"Nobody sent us." He licked his lips. "We just... heard there was a reward."

That should have been funny. Brennic had been worth a reward for years and it had never once kept him up at night, but the man wasn't looking at him. He was looking past him, at the stairs, at the room at the top of them where the girl was supposed to be asleep.

Oh, gods. No.

Behind the bar the publican rose from where he'd been crouching, set down the cleaver he hadn't used, and picked up a glass and a rag.

"You'll be paying for that window," he said.
