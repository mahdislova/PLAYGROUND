Nobody wanted to sleep, so they sat around the fire and argued about the dagger instead. It lay on a square of oilcloth between them where Vasko had set it down, and the firelight ran along it like water. Long and narrow, the blade fullered down two-thirds of its length, a crossguard that swept forward in two little horns. The grip was bound in silver wire so fine it looked like thread.

"Thirteenth Dynasty," said the priest. "Court work. See the horns on the guard? They stopped making them like that after the Saltmarch wars, because the horns caught in mail and snapped off."

"Or someone made it to look old," said Ilse. Didn't look up from the bolt she was fletching, either, which was Ilse's way of saying she'd been listening the whole time.

"Someone could have," the priest agreed. "But they'd have had to know about the horns. And nobody does, except old men like me, and dealers, and -" He stopped.

"And?"

"And the people who forge for dealers."

The elder Pellow leaned in to look and his brother leaned with him, so that for a moment their heads nearly touched. "Worth what, then? If it's real."

"If it's real," said the priest, "it's worth more than this whole company. If it's real, it belonged to the Margravine Ysolt, who had her husband killed with it at their wedding feast and then wore it on her belt for thirty years, so everyone would remember."

"Charming," said Mabb.

"It was a different time," said the priest.

"It's the same time," said Mabb, poking the fire until it spat. "People just used to be more honest about it."

Vasko picked the dagger up, which made the priest flinch, and turned it this way and that in the firelight, slow and careful, the way the old fence in Coldharbour had taught him to turn anything he was about to be lied to about. Along the base of the blade, half hidden under the guard, was a maker's mark: a little hammer struck once into the steel. He had seen that mark before. Not in a book. Last winter, on a bench in a back room in Coldharbour, beside a jar of acid and a box of fake rust.

Set the dagger back down on the oilcloth very carefully. Smiled at the priest. "Thirteenth Dynasty," he said. "You're sure."

The priest smiled back. Neither of them blinked.
